Difficulty Budgets and Composition

Expanded from the shipped Documentation~/ApiReference.md.

The budget idea

Hand-built wave lists encode difficulty by accident: wave 7 is hard because the list someone typed happens to be long. In WaveDirector Pro you set what a wave may cost, and the composer casts the wave to fit. Difficulty becomes a decision you make once, as numbers, instead of a side effect of list length.

DifficultySettings

FieldMeaning
ModeFlat, Linear, or Exponential
BaseMultiplierMultiplier applied to every wave's base budget
GrowthPerWavePer-wave growth for Linear and Exponential modes
IntervalScalePerWavePer-wave spawn-interval compression

Budgets scale as floor(base × multiplier), in integer-exact math. Spawn intervals compress per wave toward a floor of 0.05 seconds — pacing can get fierce, never broken. A wave's BudgetOverride (above 0) replaces the scaled budget entirely and is used unscaled: an authored hard number beats the curve, on purpose, for the waves you want pinned (a tuned boss night, a deliberate breather).

How the composer casts (Budget mode)

Composition is a pure, deterministic pass: given a set, a definition, a wave index, and a seed, it produces the spawn queue.

  1. Work out the wave's effective budget (scaled, or the override).
  2. Repeatedly pick, by seeded weighted choice, among archetypes that are eligible (their MinWave has been reached) and affordable (their cost fits the remaining budget).
  3. Stop when the remainder buys nothing more, or the wave's MaxSpawns cap hits.

The result reports the queue, the total cost, the unspent budget, and flags — including whether the spawn cap stopped the cast. Because the unspent remainder is always less than the cheapest affordable eligible archetype (unless the cap stopped it), a budget is genuinely spent, not approximated.

Explicit mode

When a wave must be exactly what you authored, use Explicit mode: the entry list expands to exactly those counts, and is never truncated by caps. Turn on ShuffleExplicit and the same multiset is dealt in a seed-determined order — variety without losing the authored counts.

Empty casts are reported, never silent

If no archetype is affordable for a budget — or the roster is empty — the queue comes back empty with a NoAffordableArchetype flag. The engine raises its warning event and completes the wave immediately rather than hanging, and the editor validator flags the same condition while you are authoring (page 08). A wave that casts nothing is a number problem, and the product tells you which number.
