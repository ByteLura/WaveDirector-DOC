Honest Limits: What v1 Is Not

This page carries the shipped Documentation~/WhatV1IsNot.md in full, in the same words the product uses. The limits are printed so they are a feature of the documentation, not a surprise in a review.

  • Not an enemy system. No enemy movement, health, or damage ships in the runtime, and no enemy behaviour logic of any kind. The director casts and paces; your game plays the enemies. The demo's marcher is sample content, deliberately minimal.
  • Not a networking product. Hooks only: a spawn-authority flag, events, and a wave snapshot your own networking can carry. No transport, no prediction, no lobbies — and snapshot sync aligns wave and phase, it does not reconstruct individual already-spawned enemies.
  • Not adaptive. The director never reads player performance and never changes a budget at runtime. Difficulty is the authored curve, executed exactly — that predictability is the point.
  • No enemy stat scaling. Waves scale budgets and spawn intervals. Buffing enemy health or damage per wave is your game's job, driven by the wave events if you want it.
  • No quest or narrative encounters. Survival, tower-defence, and horde patterns are the v1 genres. Dialogue-triggered or branching encounter systems are out of scope.
  • No render-pipeline content. The runtime is pipeline-agnostic and claims nothing about pipelines; the demo's materials use whichever lit shader your project provides.
  • Pooling is a separate product. The pooled spawning path works through an optional bridge to SpawnPool Pro, which is a separate purchase and is not included in this package. Out of the box, and by default, spawning runs on the built-in standalone spawner — see page 07.

Design fences worth knowing

  • The package declares no package dependencies, and the pooled bridge holds no compile-time reference in either direction: installing or removing either package cannot break the other.
  • The runtime makes no network calls, collects no telemetry, and writes no project data to disk.
  • The Runtime assembly holds no editor references; everything under the Tools menu is editor-only and never reaches a player build.
